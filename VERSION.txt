v00.01.00a - Tactical rebuild foundation
- New rebuild branch starts from the last pre-full-scale tactical baseline: v00.00.09j commit b9e5f520abe9a8306b76d79df85f79b0a20e6fe8.
- v00.00.09l5 remains preserved separately as failed QA evidence and is not used as the runtime base.
- No 09k-09l5 Company/full-scale compatibility layers are carried into the new branch.
- Rebuild architecture rule: Company is the first-class infantry tactical entity.
- OOB parent relationships are data only and must never require Transform parenting.
- One tactical entity has exactly one movement/steering owner and one combat owner.
- Regiment/Battalion/Brigade issue intent and aggregate state; they do not directly overwrite Company transforms each frame.
- 1:1 visual soldiers remain the target, implemented through GPU instancing/LOD rather than one heavy GameObject per soldier.
- First QA scale is intentionally small: one battalion per side, four Companies per battalion, approximately 180-220 men per Company.
- Brigade AI, artillery and cavalry remain disabled until Company selection, movement, formation geometry and combat pass their gates.
- Existing v00.00.09j systems are reference/baseline code only and will be replaced or retained selectively; they must not become parallel authority layers.

Planned gates
A. Stable UnitID + OOB data model.
B. Independent Company tactical entities + click/box selection.
C. 1:1 Company-driven GPU renderer.
D. Company movement/navigation with one steering owner, including hard/soft obstacles and bridge/river constraints.
E. Company combat: range, LOS, ammo, fire, reload, casualties, morale/cohesion.
F. Scale from one battalion to one full Regiment per side.
G. Battalion/Regiment HQ and Officer AI.
H. Cavalry/dragoons and artillery as first-class tactical unit types.
I. Brigade HQ + reserve/commit AI and second Regiment.
J. OOB Designer + campaign-map synchronization.

Acceptance rule
- No gate is promoted until the previous gate compiles and passes focused QA.
- Do not solve ownership conflicts with later execution-order patches. Fix the owning architecture instead.
